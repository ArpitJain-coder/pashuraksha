# 09 · Content Guide

Every word in this product is read by someone who is worried about an animal that is part of their income. That single fact settles most writing arguments before they start.

---

## 1. Who is reading

| | Arpit (farmer) | Dr. Deshmukh (vet) | Dr. Kulkarni (officer) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Reads in | Marathi | Marathi + English | English |
| Reading while | Standing in a shed at 6am, one hand on a rope | Between farms, in a jeep | At a desk, accountable to a Commissioner |
| Wants | To know if this can wait | To know if this needs him | To know if this is starting |
| Punished by | Jargon, hedging, and being made to feel foolish | Padding, and being told what he already knows | Vagueness he cannot defend upward |

**One voice across all three.** Plain, direct, unhurried. What changes between them is vocabulary, not personality: the farmer gets "a runny nose", the vet gets "nasal discharge", and both get the same respect.

---

## 2. Voice

**We sound like a competent neighbour who happens to know veterinary medicine.** Not a government form. Not a chatbot. Not a friend — a farmer with a sick animal does not need a friend, they need someone who knows what to do and says so.

| We are | We are not |
|---|---|
| Plain | Simplified. Farmers are not children; they run a business with live inventory. |
| Direct | Blunt. "Separate her from the others" is direct. "Your animal may die" is blunt and useless. |
| Certain where we can be | Falsely confident. We say what we do not know, out loud. |
| Warm through usefulness | Warm through tone. No cheerfulness near a sick animal. |
| Brief | Clipped. Brevity that costs comprehension is not brevity. |

### The tone scale
Tone moves with stakes; voice never does.

```
routine ─────────────────────────────────────────────► emergency
"Her booster is 23 days late."   "Move Lakshmi away from the other five."
warm, unhurried, offers a choice      short, imperative, one action, no choice
```

## 3. Rules

**1 · Verbs, not nouns.**
✅ "Move Lakshmi away from the other five."  ❌ "Isolation is recommended."

**2 · Second person, present tense.**
✅ "You reported this two days ago."  ❌ "Report submitted 2 days prior."

**3 · Never abbreviate a clinical term the farmer has not been taught.**
✅ "a possible respiratory infection"  ❌ "susp. HS"
The vet console is the opposite: he *has* been taught, and spelling it out wastes his time.

**4 · Familiar units only.** Litres, days, rupees, number of animals, kilometres. Never percentiles, z-scores or confidence intervals in farmer copy. One percentage is allowed — "32% under her own average" — because a milk drop against a known baseline is a quantity farmers already reason about.

**5 · State uncertainty; do not hedge into vagueness.**
✅ "We are less sure because there were no photos."
❌ "This may possibly indicate potential concerns."
Hedging reads as evasion. Naming the gap reads as honesty, and it is the only thing that makes the confident statements believable.

**6 · No exclamation marks. Anywhere.** There is no sentence in this product improved by one.

**7 · Error copy names the cause and the recovery, in that order.**
✅ "We could not refresh. The server did not answer. You are looking at what was saved at 7:12 this morning — all of it still works."
❌ "Something went wrong. Please try again."

**8 · Never blame the user, and never blame the network as if it were their fault.**
✅ "No signal — everything still works."
❌ "You are offline. Some features are unavailable."

**9 · Say what happens next.** A confirmation that ends the conversation is a dead end. "Sent to Dr. Deshmukh. He usually replies within three hours" beats "Request submitted."

**10 · Silence is copy.** A healthy animal gets no label. Three rows saying "Normal" is three rows of noise that hide the one row that matters.

**11 · Marathi is the source, English is the translation.** English-first copy produces Marathi that is grammatically correct and tonally wrong — too formal, too governmental. Draft the intent, write the Marathi, then write English that matches *it*.

**12 · Never write a sentence a farmer would not say out loud.** Read it aloud in the shed. If it sounds like a form, rewrite it.

## 4. Words we use, and words we do not

| Instead of | Write |
|---|---|
| Livestock, bovine | Your animals, her, the herd |
| Symptom | What you noticed |
| Submit / Submit report | Send |
| Alert generated | We will tell you |
| Isolate the animal | Move her away from the others |
| Preliminary diagnosis | What we think |
| Confidence score: 82% | How sure we are · 82% |
| Escalate to veterinarian | Ask for a veterinarian |
| Vaccination overdue | Her booster was due 23 days ago |
| Sync failed | It did not send |
| Offline mode | No signal |
| Data privacy policy | Who sees your reports |
| No records found | Nothing here |
| Optional | *(delete the word — say what skipping costs instead)* |

**"Optional" is banned.** It tells the user nothing about the trade. The photo step says: *"That is fine. It means we can say less — so we will ask a veterinarian sooner."* Now they can actually decide.

## 5. The five moments that carry the product

Everything else is scaffolding. These five are worth arguing over.

### 5.1 The verdict
The screen a farmer reaches after a minute of worry. It must answer *"what do I do in the next two hours"* before it answers anything else.

> **Do this first**
> **Move Lakshmi away from the other five.**
> Any corner or shed will do. Give her a separate bucket for feed and water, and wash your hands after handling her. Nothing else you do today matters as much as this.

Why it works: one action, named animal, no clinical vocabulary, a reason to believe ("nothing else matters as much"), and it is achievable with no money, no signal and no help.

### 5.2 The disclaimer
Legally required, and the fastest way to lose trust if written like a lawyer.

> **This is not a diagnosis.** It is a first read, to help you act fast. Only a veterinarian can tell you what the illness actually is. If this is an emergency, call **1962**.

Four sentences: what it is not, what it is, who can, what to do right now. No "we accept no liability".

### 5.3 The privacy promise
The single biggest adoption blocker is the fear that reporting a sick animal brings an inspector or a culling order. That fear is not addressed by a policy link.

> Reporting a sick animal has never brought an inspection or a culling order through this app. It is not built to, and that is deliberate.

Plain, absolute, falsifiable. The screen above it lists who sees what, with the "no" rows written as flatly as the "yes" rows.

### 5.4 The death interrupt
Nobody should tap through four screens of symptom pickers when an animal has already died.

> **An animal has died**
> We have stopped the questions. A veterinarian is being alerted right now, and the block office has been told.
> Do not move the carcass. Keep the others away from that spot, and away from the same water.

Past tense for what we did, imperative for what they must do. No apology — an apology here would be about us.

### 5.5 The vet's guardrail
The screen that keeps veterinarians using the queue.

> Cases you closed as "nothing needed". The red mark is the 25% limit. Past it, we are sending you too much — and we retune the sorting, rather than asking you to put up with it.

The last clause is the whole message: *the system adapts to you, not the reverse.* Most government software says the opposite by saying nothing.

## 6. Microcopy patterns

| Pattern | Rule | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Buttons** | Verb + object. Never "OK", "Submit", "Done". | "Send the request", "Open the case", "Show me the four steps" |
| **Empty — first run** | Name the payoff, not the absence. | "Scan her ear tag once. After that her vaccination card, her milk record and everything a veterinarian has ever noted live here — so you never go hunting for paper again." |
| **Empty — good news** | Positively framed, and clearly different from a first-run empty. | "Nothing needs you today. All six are eating, and the milk is where it should be. We will tell you the moment that changes." |
| **Empty — filtered** | Name the filter. Offer the exit. | "Nothing here. No animal matches 'Vaccines due'." → *Clear the filter* |
| **Loading — deliberate** | Say what is happening, in order. | "Reading what you sent" → "Checking Lakshmi's record" → "Comparing 47 reports around Wadgaon" |
| **Toast** | Past tense, plus the consequence. | "Sent for a veterinarian to look at. Thank you." |
| **Offline pill** | Reassure, then explain, then stop. | "No signal. Your reports are saved on this phone." |
| **Destructive** | Say what is lost, not "are you sure". | *(none in V1 — nothing is deletable, deliberately)* |

## 7. Localisation notes

- **Devanagari renders ~15% smaller** than Latin at the same point size. Marathi strings get a +1sp step (implemented as `.lang-mr` overrides).
- **Uppercase is a no-op in Devanagari.** Section overlines rely on size and colour there, which is why the overline is the lightest colour in the type system rather than merely the smallest.
- **Marathi is longer than English** by roughly 10–20% for equivalent meaning. Every button and chip is tested at the longest string, not the English one.
- **Do not translate the tag number format.** `274 8891 0034` is what is printed on the ear tag; changing digit grouping breaks recognition.
- **1962 stays 1962** in every language. It is a phone number, not a word.
- **Voice input is a peer, not a fallback.** Its label is "Say it instead", never "Accessibility options".

## 8. What is still unwritten

Honest gaps, so nobody assumes this is finished:

- **Hindi is stubbed.** The language picker offers it; the strings currently fall back to Marathi. It needs a native writer, not a translation pass.
- **Two pictograms are not carrying their weight** — "hard breathing" and "swollen udder" still read as abstract shapes. They need an illustrator and a round of comprehension testing with farmers who cannot read the label underneath. Everything in this guide about not making people feel foolish applies double to an icon they have to guess at.
- **IVR scripts do not exist.** The feature-phone path in [05](05-architecture-and-flows.md) needs spoken-word copy, which is a different craft from screen copy — no rereading, no scanning, and the menu depth is the whole design.
- **SMS fallbacks are unwritten.** 160 characters, no formatting, and they arrive without context.
- **Nothing here has been tested aloud with a farmer.** Every claim in this document is a hypothesis until it has been.
